## Kansō 簡素

### What it means

Simplicity: leave out what is not needed.

### How kansō applies it

One fact, one home. Nothing repeated, nothing explained twice, nothing you already know. Fewer presses.

## Fukinsei 不均斉

### What it means

Asymmetry: balance without forced symmetry.

### How kansō applies it

The window balances by weight, not by mirror: a quiet sidebar against the work. Hierarchy comes from size and weight.

## Kokō 枯高

### What it means

Austerity: only the essential, the bare core.

### How kansō applies it

One accent. State colour only on what asks for you. Exact Lucide icons, never our own.

## Shizen 自然

### What it means

Naturalness: nothing forced.

### How kansō applies it

Motion only for live work (the ensō, the shimmer). Nothing jumps or resizes. Light is paper, not white, and reads like dark.

## Yūgen 幽玄

### What it means

Subtlety: suggest rather than show everything.

### How kansō applies it

Detail appears when you open or point at something. A stand-in shows only while its home is out of view.

## Datsuzoku 脱俗

### What it means

Freedom from the conventional.

### How kansō applies it

One memorable element: the ensō, and the dawn that rises once.

## Seijaku 静寂

### What it means

Tranquility.

### How kansō applies it

A calm rest state. One drawing for each piece and each state, so nothing surprises.

## Why

- kansō is named after one of the seven principles of Zen art described by Shin'ichi Hisamatsu in *Zen and the Fine Arts* (1971). The design follows all seven as one philosophy, not taste.
- Every guideline names the principle it serves. A decision that cannot name one is an opinion, and is not taken.
- One limit sits above them: kansō removes information, never options. Every action stays one press away.
